    Evaluation and budgets¶

    Status: Shipped.

    A Task declares the evaluator, artifact representation, objectives, and optional budget ceiling. The evaluator—not the candidate—owns what better means.

    import meta_evolve as meta

task = meta.Task(
    evaluator=lambda text: {"quality": len(set(text.split()))},
    objectives=(meta.Maximize("quality"),),
    budget=meta.Budget(evaluations=6, trials=5),
)

    Maximize and Minimize identify ordered objectives. A run-level budget may narrow the task ceiling but cannot widen it.

    Task.environment is an immutable evaluator-owned declaration. Its empty default preserves ordinary runs; when supplied it contributes to task and run identity, so changing a nested episode environment invalidates its RunId.

    Exact accounting¶

    Seed evaluation consumes one evaluation and no successor trial. Each attempted successor consumes one trial; a valid evaluated successor consumes one evaluation. Token, monetary-spend, and wall usage come from the component action that actually performed the work. Usage is immutable and aggregated in the run summary.

    Run.summary() raises NoSuccessfulEvaluation when a run scores nothing, so it is the wrong accessor whenever you are accumulating usage across nested runs — one failing inner run would unwind the caller and discard everything banked before it. Call run.usage() there: same figures, and it never raises for that reason.

    Rolling a nested run's usage into an outer one also means dropping its counts, since trials and evaluations belong to the run that minted them. Usage names that projection, so you never enumerate the fields yourself:

    total += inner.usage().without_counts

    run.inspect().overview.task_usage reports the same figure and remains available, but it builds a whole aggregate to reach one field; prefer the accessor.

    Search checks the effective budget before starting more work — for counts, tokens, and wall time. Monetary spend is knowable only once a call returns, so it is enforced post-hoc instead. Exhaustion is a recorded stop reason. Components never grant themselves additional work.

    Money¶

    Usage.spend_micros records charged monetary spend in integer micro-USD (1 unit = one millionth of a US dollar). A wrapper reports the amount its provider actually billed; estimates keep their status and price basis in evidence instead of appearing as exact bills. Core never infers money from tokens and never converts currencies.

    Token-only telemetry is not by itself evidence of a zero bill. Reporting zero is correct when the wrapper's own auth arrangement establishes that no per-call charge exists — a subscription-backed client, say — and the absence of a cost field then has nothing to hide. When paid work returns no readable exact charge, the amount is unknown rather than zero, and the wrapper raises ExecutionAccountingUnknown; see the three cases in SDK integration.

    Budget(spend_micros=...) sets a ceiling with post-hoc enforcement: spend is known only after a call returns, so the first action that crosses the ceiling is billed, recorded as a typed ResourceExhausted failure, and halts the run. There is no hard cap — reservation-based preflight is future work. A ceiling of 0 means free work only: unpaid components run normally, and the first paid action fails typed and stops the run.

    A ceiling can also sit on an individual trial. When a trial spends past its own spend_micros, the run halts — including for subsequent free work, since a breached ceiling is a property of the run's history, not of the next action. A trial with no ceiling anywhere, neither aggregate nor per-trial, stays unbounded, exactly as trials=None means unlimited trials.

    spend_micros is wrapper-self-reported and not independently verified by the framework: a wrapper that omits or understates its provider's charge understates trusted accounting, and the round-up conversion convention is advisory. The framework guarantees exact aggregation of what was reported, not the truth of the report.

    Enforcement bounds the framework's paid actions, not a provider's billing. Under durable execution a crash between a paid call and its commit leaves the charge real but unrecorded, and recovery re-executes the call; bounding that requires provider-side idempotency, which is future work.

    Evidence and uncertainty¶

    An evaluation can return scalar or objective-keyed metrics, uncertainty, immutable evidence, and action usage. Evidence records what was observed; it is not a candidate-controlled score. External evaluators also record environment and bounded process evidence where available.

    Typed failures¶

    Invalid candidate shape, proposer failure, evaluator failure, timeout, resource exhaustion, policy violation, and infrastructure failure retain their distinct types and details. A trial with no valid evaluation is non-rankable. It never becomes zero, negative infinity, or a fabricated metric.

    Run-level capability and durability errors use the shipped error hierarchy, including CapabilityError, BudgetUnavailable, and InvalidDecision.

    Authority¶

    The candidate may propose artifact content and declare causal observations. The task/evaluator owns hidden data, environment, metrics, and failure translation; the coordinator owns effective budgets and commitment; governance owns permissions and promotion.
